## 1. Where it stands

### 1.1 One paragraph

dyna.ink (the repository and code still say MetaMedium) is a canvas you draw on
with a pen or a mouse. The engine reads each mark as it is drawn: what shape it
is, what part it plays in the drawing, and what the drawing is (a flowchart, a
class diagram, a molecule, a page of maths, a pattern piece). You hold some
marks and a field opens beside your hand. It offers what those marks can
become: drawn clean, tidied, written as Mermaid, solved, a page, a program, a
named thing the canvas learns. Everything the engine knows answers instantly
with no model. A model is asked only when you ask, and only for what the engine
cannot do:
- reading handwriting;
- saying what a group is;
- writing a page or a program;
- choosing between two equal matches.

The model can be Claude Code at your desk, a local model, or a hosted one.
Boards are kept on the device, many of them, and nothing is lost. They work
with an Apple Pencil on an iPad, can be shared live with another hand
(Claude's included), and can be searched.

### 1.4 What is built — the layers

The order is bottom to top. Each layer has its section in `CLAUDE.md`.

1. **Ink and the log.**
   - A board *is* its log of events, one per line. State is replayed from it.
   - Undo takes back this hand's own last act.
   - Versioned files (R2), a journal per board in the browser (R3), many boards with a trash (R1).
2. **Readings.**
   - The shape rung reads eight shapes.
   - The diagram rung gives six roles.
   - Relations, concepts, and words gathered from letters.
   - Clean forms, drawn at the angle the box was drawn.
3. **Notations** (what a drawing *is*):
   - flowchart;
   - UML class diagram;
   - sequence diagram, with dashed lines;
   - state diagram;
   - ER diagram;
   - mind map;
   - garment pattern piece;
   - and, since 2 Oct, **a diagram**: the fallback for nodes joined by connectors tied at both ends.

   Mermaid goes out and comes back in, with a layered layout. Connectors can be
   routed at right angles, and *Tidy the diagram* lines the ranks up.
4. **Editing.**
   - Handles reshape one mark's clean form.
   - Magnets tie a connector's ends to sites.
   - Tied connectors follow what they are tied to. This is derived, never logged.
5. **Maths.**
   - Numbers beside a figure are read and solved figure by figure, with no model.
   - A page of steps is checked line by line; a typed `=` works out a sum.
   - True-size SVG, and tiled print with a test square.
   - A garment piece is read with its grain line, notches, dart and seam allowance.
6. **Tools.** Every affordance in the field is an offer from one of 25
   registered tools (`metamedium-core/src/tools/`).
7. **Context.** What stands beside the hand can lift an offer, never hide one.
   The pill's tooltip says why it was lifted, and the top offer holds steady.
8. **Library packs.** Premade definitions and notations a board uses by an
   event: `basics@1` (bubble, molecule), the seven notation packs.
9. **Seats** (who is asked, per job), each with its own model:
   - *reader*: handwriting into text;
   - *writer*: briefs, pages, programs, *What is this?*;
   - *decider*: Jev, *Which is it?* on a tie;
   - *semantic*: on the device, for meaning.

   The key is the provider's, entered once and used by every seat on that
   provider. Claude Code can take the reader and writer jobs over MCP.
10. **The iPad.**
    - Pencil, finger and palm.
    - Pictures kept and drawn under the ink.
    - Exports that carry the pictures (`.dyna.zip`, SVG, PNG, PDF).
    - Regions; Find across every board, with thumbnails.
    - Persistent storage asked for once; installable to the Home Screen with icons.
11. **Live rooms.**
    - Another tab, another machine, or Claude's hand, in one room through a relay.
    - Each hand's ink is in its own colour; a word goes only on your own ink.
    - Pictures travel by their hash.
    - Claude may move anyone's marks, and says whose it moved (A2b).
12. **The field, up to par** (2 Oct, PLAN-FIELD-PAR):
    - your own words find the act; what is missing is said; `?` lists everything;
    - the board's acts are reachable from the field;
    - a lone word waits for a pick;
    - *Name it* and *Write “…” on it* are folded into one where they would do the same;
    - matching by meaning while typing, when the semantic seat is held.

## 2. The affordance guide

Every way a hand can act, where it is reached, and where it is defined. "Tool"
means a registered tool in `metamedium-core/src/tools/`. "Typed" means it is
reached by typing in the field and never shown as a pill.

### 2.1 Gestures on the canvas

| Gesture | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Draw | A stroke. Read at once (shape, role); the reading shows under the mark for a moment | `07-input.js`, core `recognition.ts` |
| Draw a loop, then the check ✓ across it | Holds what the loop encloses and opens the field. The loop is plain ink until the check | `session.ts` (summon), `BUILTIN_COMMAND_MARK` |
| Double-tap inside a waiting loop | The same as the check, with no mark to draw | `summonHeld` |
| The check alone, near recent marks | Holds what it crosses and what was drawn with it just now (it reads backwards) | `Summon.scopeSource` |
| Press and hold a mark | Holds it with everything it hangs together with; the field opens | `holdAround` in `07-input.js` |
| Scratch back and forth across a mark three times | Erases it. Twice says *one more pass erases it*. A closed stroke is never a scratch; an arrowhead arriving at a mark is not a scratch | `session/erase.ts` |
| Tap off an open field | Closes it and leaves no dot, even with a few pixels of wobble | `07-hand.js` `releaseIs` |
| Double-click empty ground | A text editor where the text will stand | `19-text.js` |
| End a line or arrow on a mark | The pen feels the site (a ring) and ties the end there. **An arrow ties at its tip** even though the pen lifts at the barb (FP3) | `05-snap.js`, `arrowTipMagnet` in `07-input.js` |
| Drag inside a selection, a corner, or the knob | Move, scale, turn. One event, the ink untouched | `05-selection.js` |
| Drag a ring on one held mark | Reshapes its clean form. The ink stays; tied connectors follow | core `session/handles.ts` |
| Drag a region's title | Moves the region and everything it holds, in one act | `12-regions.js` |
| Pencil / finger / palm | Before a pen is seen, one finger draws. After it, the pen draws, one finger pans, two pinch, and a palm is nothing | `07-hand.js` |
| Pencil hover | The reading of the mark under it, and the magnet in reach | `penHover` |
| Scroll / pinch | Pan / zoom | `01-view.js` |
| Draw your own mark five times | Teaches it as your command mark (*controls › Helpers › mark*) | `03-teach.js` |
| Tap a reading's chip beside a group | Opens the field on those marks again | `08-render.js` |

### 2.2 The field — its anatomy, and what Enter does

The field opens beside the hand when marks are held. From the top:

1. **The input**, and under it **the reading line**: what Enter will do, said
   before it is pressed. When Enter would do nothing, the line is grey.
2. **Four round core buttons**, always in the same slots: **Name · Copy ·
   Paste · Erase**.
3. **What this is** — the readings, each with its number:
   - a name the library knows (*molecule 0.92 · Basics*);
   - a notation (*a flowchart 0.84*, *a diagram 0.70*);
   - a concept (*row 0.83*, *flow 0.90*);
   - a model's reading, with its author (*gate 0.70 · claude*);
   - writing (*writing 0.75*).

   A tap on a reading takes it as the name, except a notation, which writes its
   Mermaid or says what it saw.
4. **What it affords** — the offers, ranked by what they stand on, by use, and
   by what stands beside the marks. A pill with a dot asks a model; its tooltip
   is its reason. When there are many, the rest are *a keystroke away*.

**What Enter does.** The reader, `readFieldCommand` in `09-field.js`, is pure
and tested in Node. It reads the typed text in this order and stops at the
first match:

| # | Typed | Enter does | Line says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | nothing | the likely act, the first in the ranked order (*Draw them clean*, *read it* for writing) — never a reading taken as a name | `↵ Draw them clean` |
| 2 | `?` | nothing; every offer is shown, the typed-only ones too | `↵ all these marks can do — tap one, or type its name` |
| 3 | `= 24 ÷ 3` | puts the sum and its result on the board as text | `↵ 24 ÷ 3 = 8 — put it on the board as text` |
| 4 | a prefix: `name:` `label:` `region:` `ask:` `draw:` `page:` `run:` `new:` `what:` | that act | e.g. `↵ write “inlet” on it` |
| 5 | an offer's verb or label start, **including the words a person says for it** (`tools/intents.ts`) | takes it | `↵ Tidy the diagram` |
| 6 | a library entry's name | reuses its program here; no model asked | `↵ … — from the library` |
| 7 | words a definition can be told (*flees anything bigger*) | the behaviour | `↵ fish: flees …` |
| 8 | a board act: `export`, `find pricing`, `print`, `examples`, `help` (≤ 3 words) | opens that pane; nothing written | `↵ export the board` |
| 9 | an act these marks lack (≤ 3 words that are, or lead with, its word) | **nothing** — quietly says what is missing | `↵ Make it Mermaid — hold two marks or more — one mark is no diagram` |
| 10 | words the semantic seat found near an offered act (seat held, typing rested) | takes it | `↵ Make it Mermaid — by meaning 0.71` |
| 11 | a word or two that names nothing | **nothing** — waits for a tap on *Name it* or *Write … on it* | `↵ a word — tap one: a name, or words on them` |
| 12 | a sentence (three words or more) | a brief: a page or a program; the boxes stand at once, and a model writes the words | `↵ the page’s boxes at once, then qwen writes the words` |

With no model joined, a brief that needs one is kept: it runs when a model
joins, and the field says so.

**Intent words** (`tools/intents.ts`, one home): the words a person says for
each act. For example *diagram*, *mermaid* and *chart* find Make it Mermaid;
*tidy*, *line up*, *arrange* and *straighten* find Tidy the diagram; *connect*
and *route* find Route the connectors. When an act is not offered, the table
also holds what it is missing, said in words. `missingFor` and `hostIntentOf`
read only three words or fewer that are, or lead with, the act's word, so a
sentence stays a brief.

**The class rule** (John, 2 Oct): *we will have expandable classes of things.*
A drawing can belong to a class:
- *a diagram* is the class a fallback names: nodes joined by tied connectors,
  when nothing says more;
- *a molecule* (all circles) is its own class, read by its pack.

A new class claims its drawings the same way: a notation, or a pack's
definition.

## 3. The user guide

`HELP.md` is the in-app help page and the full reference; it is what the *help*
tile shows. This part walks the common tasks end to end, with what you should
see at each step.

### 3.1 First minute

1. Open `/app/`. The board is empty. The panel says *draw a few marks → press
   and hold one → choose what it becomes*, with **start from an example**.
2. Tap **start from an example** for a board of your own holding a molecule
   (the Basics pack). Or open **boards › examples** for a flowchart, a class
   diagram or a pattern page. The example itself never changes.
3. On an iPad, **add it to the Home Screen** first (Safari › Share › Add to
   Home Screen). An installed app is not cleared after a week. Boards kept in a
   Safari tab do not show in the installed app: move them with **export ›
   board + pictures**, then **boards › from a file…** in the app.

### 3.2 Draw, hold, choose

1. Draw a few marks. Each one's reading shows under it for a moment.
2. Press and hold one, or circle them and draw a ✓ across the loop (or
   double-tap inside it). The marks are held, and the field opens by your hand.
3. Read the line under the input: it is what **Enter** will do. Tap a pill, or
   type what you want in your own words. Type **?** to see everything.
4. **Undo** takes back your own last act: one stroke, or everything one choice
   did.

### 3.3 A diagram (John's case, 2 Oct)

1. Draw two boxes. Draw a line **from one box onto the other**. A ring shows
   where the pen will tie, and the end ties when you lift there. An arrow ties
   where its head points, even if you lift at a barb.
2. Circle all three and ✓. You should see:
   - *what this is*: **a diagram 0.70**;
   - *affords*: **Make it Mermaid**, **Tidy the diagram**, **Route the
     connectors**.
3. Type *diagram*: **↵ Make it Mermaid**. Type *tidy*: **↵ Tidy the diagram**.
   Type *connect*: **↵ Route the connectors**.
4. Draw with arrows, diamonds and so on, and it reads as **a flowchart** (or a
   class, sequence, state or ER diagram, or a mind map). A diagram type that
   reads above its threshold replaces *a diagram*.
5. **Make it Mermaid**: the text stands beside the drawing, drawn as a diagram.
   **Draw it** on a Mermaid text puts it back as marks.
6. Circles joined by lines are a **molecule**, not a diagram: they get *Show it
   in 3D* and, with the Basics pack, the name *molecule*.

### 3.4 A word: name it, or write it on

Type one or two words with marks held, *banana* say. **Enter waits.** The
field offers:
- **Name it “banana”** · *finds more like it*: the marks become one thing the
  canvas learns, and the next drawing like it is offered as *banana*;
- **Write “banana” on it** · *only the words*: the word is written on your own
  ink as a caption, and nothing is made.

Tap one. Only one pill shows when only one would act, for example a thing you
already named, held alone, or marks that are all someone else's. `name: …` and
`label: …` say which, up front.

### 3.5 Notes on an iPad

1. Write with the pencil; a finger pans, and a palm is ignored.
2. **Pictures**: *controls › Board › import*, with *photos* (several), *camera*
   or *other files*. Or drop or paste them. Each picture is kept and drawn
   under the ink, so you can write on a photo. *Trace into ink* turns a
   drawing's lines into marks.
3. **Regions**: draw a big rectangle round notes, hold them all, and choose
   *Make it a region* (or type `region: Monday`). Drag the title to move the
   region and everything in it. The panel's outline lists the regions.
4. **Read my notes**: circle several lines and press Enter (*read these*). The
   lines are read in one batch by your reader seat, and the words land beside
   your ink. *Read the board* reads every line. *Read the picture* reads a
   photo of a page into a text beside it.
5. **Find**: `/` or the *find* button searches every board as you type. With
   the semantic seat loaded, it also searches by meaning.

### 3.6 Numbers

Write `24` and `8` beside a right triangle's legs: *25.30* stands beside the
long side. Then:
- A page of steps gets ✓ or ✗ at each line.
- `= A ÷ 3` in the field works out a sum against the page.
- **Print at true size** (or *export*) draws the figure from its numbers at
  real size, tiled onto pages with a 1 in / 2 cm test square on each.
- A **pattern piece** with a grain line, notches, dart and seam allowance says
  what it is cut at and sewn at.

### 3.7 Models, Claude, and the seats

- Nothing asks a model until you ask: *What is this?*, *Read the writing*, a
  sentence typed as a brief, `ask:`, `draw:`.
- **controls › Helpers › models**:
  - **Claude Code — in this room** comes first, when Claude's hand is in the
    room (*live › with Claude*);
  - then the seats: **reader** (sees handwriting), **writer**, **decider**
    (Jev), **semantic** (on this device; tap *load it here* once, about 30 MB);
  - then local models (Ollama, LM Studio), and hosted ones by key.

  A key is the provider's, typed once, and stays on the device only if you
  tick *remember*.
- An ask with no model that can answer it is **kept**, and runs when one joins.
- **Claude may move your marks** to organise them. The status line says so
  (*claude moved 2 of your marks*). Your undo does not reach Claude's move:
  move them back yourself, or ask Claude to.

### 3.8 Boards, export, rooms

- **boards** has new, from a file, rename, duplicate, delete (to the trash,
  restorable), and examples. *Reset* puts the old board in the trash.
- **export** writes:
  - **board + pictures** (`.dyna.zip`, opens whole on another device);
  - **board.svg / .png / .pdf** (what is held, else the board);
  - the log (`canvas.jsonl`);
  - Mermaid, when what is held reads as a diagram;
  - true size and print, when a figure has numbers and a unit.

  Typing *export* in the field opens this pane.
- **live**: a room name, for tabs on this machine or, through a relay, other
  machines. Each hand's ink is in its own colour; a word goes only on your
  own ink.

## 4. The agent guide

### 4.1 Claude as a hand on John's board

**Joining.**
- The `metamedium` MCP server (`Demos/mcp.mjs`) joins room `claude` through the
  relay on this machine (`:8020`, starting one if none answers). On dyna.ink
  it uses `https://relay.dyna.ink`, with a room key in `MM_RELAY_KEY`.
- John opens the canvas with *live › with Claude*, or
  `?live=claude&relay=http://127.0.0.1:8020`.
- If the session's tools are not loaded, run `mcp.mjs` from the shell: stdin
  fed by `tail -f` on a command file, one JSON-RPC line per call (`CLAUDE.md`,
  *The MCP hand*).

**How to act.**
- **Look first**: `canvas_look` gives the board in words with ids, regions and
  handwriting lines. Use `canvas_see` when the ink matters (handwriting, a
  sketch, a picture).
- **Propose; never bless.** Every reading you give is held and attributed with
  a confidence and a reason, and John decides. Never claim a reading is
  settled.
- **Labels go only on your own ink** (`canvas_label`). Rename only a region you
  made.
- **You may move anything, John's marks included** (his decision, 2 Oct, A2b).
  Say whose marks you moved, in your own words, and offer to move them back:
  his undo does not reach your move. Reverse a move with the opposite `dx, dy`.
- You can write code (`canvas_write`) but **cannot play it**. You hold no keys.

**Being the seat.** When John asks *Claude Code (MCP hand)* (*What is this?*,
*Read the writing*, `ask:`, a brief), the page parks a brief in the room.
1. `node Demos/seat-watch.mjs` under the Monitor tool prints one line per brief
   parked; that line is what wakes the session.
2. `canvas_pending` gives the brief, the marks, the contract and, for a read,
   the ink as a picture.
3. `canvas_answer {key, reply}` replies in the contract:
   - *What is this?*: `[{"label","confidence","reasoning"}]`;
   - *Read the writing*: `[{"text","confidence"}]`;
   - a question: prose.

   Or send `{key, refuse}`. The reply is checked with the page's own parser
   before it is sent.
4. A picture cannot reach the seat yet: the log carries no pixels.

**Start here.**


**The invariants: break none.**
1. **Red first.** Write the record that fails on `master`, then the change.
2. **Never weaken an assertion.** A golden (e2e 49, the field's offers)
   changes only by design, in a commit of its own, saying what moved and why.
3. **Never edit `metamedium-core/src/session/session.scenario.test.ts`.**
4. **A model is asked only by a deliberate act.** Nothing on draw, hold,
   summon, join or field open; the gate counts calls.
5. **Keys never enter the log, the board, an export, a cache or an address.**
   The `models` scenario looks for them.
6. **Ink is never covered or replaced.** A derived form draws in front, with
   the ink beneath.
7. **State is a pure function of the log.** Derived things (bindings that
   follow, routes, regions' contents, context, indices) are never logged.
8. **One definition, one home.** Never restate a threshold in prose; cite the
   file.
9. **Update `CLAUDE.md` in the same commit as a structural change.**
10. **No real model or vendor in tests.** The gate blocks model hosts. A 403
    from the proxy is policy: report it, never route around it.

**How to add things.**
- **A tool**: one file in `src/tools/`, plus one registration line in
  `builtin.ts`, appended at the end so e2e 49's order stands. An offer is data:
  key, label, reason, base, grounds, verbs, `hidden`, `note`, `data`. Taking it
  goes through `takeOffer`, stamped as one act.
- **Words for an act**: an entry in `INTENTS` (`tools/intents.ts`), with
  `is(key)`, label, words and `missing(scope)`. Beware words that are also
  verbs, readings or names: *copy* belonged to the Copy button, *row* to the
  row reading, and *in 3d* sits inside sentences.
- **A notation**: `src/notations/<id>.ts` with a `read`, registered in
  `notation.ts`, plus a Mermaid writer and reader, a pack in
  `packs/shipped/`, and a bench test. A fallback class sets `fallback: true`
  and is said only through `sayable`.
- **A gate record**: in the scenario's module (`e2e/<name>.mjs`) or the canvas
  harness. Draw with the pointer's own input (`page.mouse`, or `t.stroke` in
  the harness), never through the session's doors when testing a hand's path.

**How work lands.**
1. Each session has one branch (here `claude/friendly-galileo-g1z8wo`).
2. Commit there, with the attribution lines the session gives.
3. Push the branch.
4. When John says *merge*, fast-forward `master` to it
   (`git push origin HEAD:master`).
5. Never a PR unless asked. Never a release unless asked.

**Traps found this cycle.**
- Agents' worktrees can start at a stale base: check `git log -1` first.
- A script that writes screenshots with a relative path writes into the repo:
  write to the scratchpad.
- WebKit is not installed in this container, and `playwright install` is not
  run: rely on CI's `webkit` job.
- The gate takes about 11 minutes. Run one gate at a time (memory).
- A check piped through `tail` exits 0 whatever the tests did. Use
  `bash -o pipefail`, or read the counts. One engine test broke this way on
  2 Oct (the placeholder page's slot wording) and reached `master`.
