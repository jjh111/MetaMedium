MetaMedium

A drawing is code. So the drawing and the code are set in the same face, on the same paper, and every colour on the surface is the engine saying what it knows.

01 Foundations

Four laws. Everything else is a consequence.

The system is small on purpose. A rule that has to be remembered is a rule that will drift, and this project already has three palettes in three surfaces because nobody wrote the rule down.

I

Paper first.

MetaMedium is a surface you draw on, so the light surface is the design and the dark surface is that same design inverted for a canvas you stare at for an hour. Dark mode changes tokens only. No rule below the token layer may branch on theme — if a component needs to know which surface it is on, the token set is wrong.

II

One face carries everything.

IBM Plex Mono sets display, prose, UI and code. A project whose whole claim is that a drawing is code cannot change voice between the drawing and the code. Register is made with size, weight, spacing and rules — never with a second typeface.

III

Colour is signal, not decoration.

Teal is the keyword. Blue means read and accepted. Grey means held, unblessed. Amber and red are confidence and damage. Purple is a model contributed this. Nothing on the surface is coloured because it looked flat, and no colour is ever applied as an accent bar — a filled chip that names a value is fine; a stripe down the side of a card that means nothing is not.

IV

Ink is never destroyed, so ink is never covered.

The engine keeps the hand's stroke under every clean form, transform and reading. The visual system honours that: a derived form draws in front at full strength with the hand's ink faint beneath it, and the ink stays visible. Anything the system inferred must be visibly reversible.

One definition, one home. Every value on this page comes from brand/tokens.css. A surface links that file or inlines it verbatim; nothing redefines a token it could import.

02 Palette

Warm paper, sea ink, one teal.

The ground keeps the whitepaper's warmth — paper that has been left in the light, not a screen pretending to be one — and the ink stays the deep sea-blue the whitepaper hero already used. The paper does the warming and the ink stays cool: a warm grey beside a teal keyword goes muddy, and the contrast between a warm ground and a cool mark is what makes ink look like ink.

Emphasis

Mono has no elegant italic at display size and no small caps, so the system does not pretend. Strong is weight 500 in full ink. Emphasis in prose is the real italic, at prose size only. A marker highlight is the reader's mark; hot is editorial urgency and is rationed to roughly once a page.

In headings, em is teal and upright — never slanted. A slanted display mono looks like a mistake.

Numbers are evidence

Every measured value is set tabular so a column of confidences can be compared down the page. Thresholds are never written into prose — they live in the engine and drift the moment a document restates them.

04 The mark

A loop and a check. The gesture is the logo.

The signet is the canonical loop with the command mark drawn across it — circle some marks, cross them with a check. It is the one gesture the whole engine is built around, so it is the one shape the brand needs. The loop is teal because it is held; the check is blue because it is the act of accepting.

Rules for the mark

  • — Medium is the teal half. Never colour Meta, never colour both.
  • — One word, no space, no hyphen: MetaMedium.
  • — Tracking is −0.035em at display size and eases to −0.02em below 1.5rem.
  • — The signet's clear space is the height of its own loop. It never sits inside a coloured plate.
  • — On ink, the loop takes teal soft and the check keeps blue. The gesture must stay legible on both surfaces.

Favicon and small sizes

Stroke weight grows as the mark shrinks. At 16px the loop is dropped and the check stands alone — a hairline circle at favicon size is mud, and the check is the half that carries meaning.

05 Ink on the canvas

Six ways a mark can look. Each one is a fact.

This is the part of the system no other styleguide has, and the part that must not drift: how a stroke renders is how the engine reports what it knows about that stroke. The hand's ink is always the darkest thing on the canvas. Anything derived draws in front of it — and the ink stays visible underneath, because undo has to be believable before it is pressed.

Hand inkWeight 2.6, full ink, round caps. The darkest mark on the surface, always.
Offer, untakenA dashed teal ghost over live ink. Dashes mean nobody has agreed to this.
Clean form, takenSolid blue in front, the hand faint beneath. Never replaces the ink — undo drops the rep.
The model's penPurple ink, same fingerprint and same eraser. Attribution is on the mark, not in a log.
Scratched outThree crossings of the target's own outline erases it. The scratch is red; the ink fades, it does not vanish mid-gesture.
Held, then crossedThe loop is dashed teal over a faint teal wash; the check that acts on it is blue. Held is never solid.

The one prohibition. No inferred form may be drawn in a way the hand's ink cannot be seen through. If a clean form, a tidy transform or a rendered artifact hides the marks that framed it, the surface has broken the engine's own contract — and a page rendering over erased ink is exactly the silent phantom the engine degrades to prevent.

06 Readings

Show the argument, not the verdict.

The engine multi-parses on principle: nothing wins by silencing the others, all tiers show at once, and disagreement between sources is the thing the human most wants to see. A UI that renders one confident label has thrown away the product. So candidates are a ranked list, confidence is a tick meter rather than a filled bar — a bar promises progress, ticks report evidence — and every row carries who said it.

07 UI

Hairlines, one radius, no shadows to speak of.

Chrome sits beside a canvas, so it stays quiet: a 1px border, a 2px radius, and a shadow so soft it only says this floats above the ink. Nothing is rounded like a pill, nothing has a gradient, and no control wears a colour it has not earned.

08 Surfaces

Warm paper, a grey room.

The repository has carried three palettes across four surfaces — warm paper with red in the whitepaper, near-black with gold in the demos, sea-deep with cyan and gold in the personal-site work. Two of those were borrowed. From here there is one system with two grounds, and the same component built once renders on both because only tokens change. The dark surface is deliberately the less colourful of the two: on a canvas you stare at for an hour, saturation belongs to the user's own marks, so the ground goes neutral grey and every claim colour is pulled well back.

Recognition · §3

Extent is the strongest single discriminator

MetaMedium · Whitepaper v5.1 · draft

A stroke arrives as a few hundred points and nothing else. Before anything can be said about what it means, something has to be measurable about what it is: how straight it runs, whether it closed, how many times it turned sharply, and what fraction of its own bounding box its outline manages to enclose.

That last figure is the one that matters. A rectangle encloses nearly all of its box, a circle about four fifths, a triangle about half — and unlike a corner count, extent holds when the hand misses a corner. Counting corners is fragile in exactly the way hands are unreliable: miss one and a box becomes a triangle, which is precisely the bug this measurement was introduced to kill.

The box is the tightest one at any angle, not the axis-aligned bounds. A hand rarely draws square to the screen — and against axis-aligned bounds, a box tilted ten degrees filled about eighty per cent of its box and lost its offer to be redrawn clean.

Two properties matter more than any individual number. Every detector that qualifies contributes a candidate, and results come back ranked — nothing wins by silencing the others. And each detector scores continuously from the evidence it found, so confidence is measured rather than assigned. Fixed confidences with overlapping bands are how a three-corner shape used to match both triangle and rectangle and win on a constant, which is not a ranking.

Specimen rules. Prose is --measure wide and no wider. A pull quote is bounded by two teal hairlines, never by a left bar. Figures sit in a paper well with a caption that names the figure in teal and the source in ink. There is no drop cap and no small-caps opener — mono has neither, and faking them is a costume.

10 Voice

Say what was measured. Then say what it cost.

The visual system and the writing make the same promise, so the voice belongs in the same document. MetaMedium writes in the past tense about things it actually ran: a claim arrives with the evidence, and a rule arrives with the failure that produced it.

The engine, reporting
“rectangle, 0.86 — extent near 1.0, four corners along the path.”
Grounded and hedged. It says what it saw, not what the thing is.
The document, explaining
“Corner count is fragile. Extent holds regardless. This is what fixed rectangles reading as triangles.”
Rule, then the bug it fixed. A rule with no failure behind it is decoration.
The surface, offering
“3 circled — cross with ✓, or draw them clean.”
Lowercase, plain, no exclamation. The canvas offers; it never congratulates.

The two lists

Do
  • Name the file that holds the number, not the number.
  • Attribute every reading to whoever made it.
  • Write “the engine cannot see that yet” rather than staying silent.
  • Keep the lowercase register of the canvas in the canvas.
  • Let two sources disagree in the open.
Don't
  • Restate a threshold in prose. It drifted in ten documents already.
  • Say “AI-powered”, “seamless”, “magic”, or “just works”.
  • Render one confident label where the engine returned four candidates.
  • Colour something to fill space, or add a bar to give a card an edge.
  • Claim a demo shows a thing the demo does not show.
11 Figures & diagrams

A diagram is drawn in the engine's own roles.

This is the section the whitepaper most needed and the system did not have. Its figures were authored for a dark board and then dropped onto light paper, so each one carries its own palette, its own type sizes and its own idea of what a caption is. The fix is not per-figure taste: a figure has one plate, one padding, one caption structure, and a diagram is drawn in the same six roles the diagram rung reads — container, node, edge, label, annotation — plus the two claims a figure can make.

One unit is one pixel

Every diagram is authored 1000 units wide. At --fig-wide (1040px) a unit is a pixel, so one type scale holds across every figure in the document. A diagram inherited at another viewBox width sets --u to width ÷ 1000 and lands on the same pixels — which is how six diagrams drawn at 400, 700, 750 and 800 units stop disagreeing about how big a label is.

Titlewhat the figure is saying, once, at the top26u · ink-3
Nodethe name of a thing in the diagram20u · 500

Microdimensions, values, asides14u
Tinythe barely-there: guides, tick labels12u · ink-4
Strokesstructure · secondary · hairline2.4 / 1.6 / 1

No italic and no bold in a diagram. Mono has one usable weight above 400 and no display italic; emphasis in a figure is made with colour and position, the same way the canvas makes it.

The roles, and the two claims

The caption rule

A caption is part of the plate: same padding as the drawing, one hairline between them, a teal micro-label naming the figure and the explanation in prose under it. Two paddings and a heavier border is what made the old captions look bolted on.

Do

figure.fig > .plate padding: --fig-pad > figcaption padding: --fig-pad border-top: 1px --rule-soft > b the label, teal, micro, caps then the explanation, --t-small

One padding token, so the caption's left edge lines up with the drawing's.

Don't

  • — Put the label and description inside the plate as plain divs. They are the caption; a screen reader should be told so.
  • — Size a figure in vw. Its relationship to the text column then changes at every window width.
  • — Tint the plate with a gradient. A drawing needs a flat ground or its own greys start lying.
  • — Set the caption in italic. At this size mono italic costs more than it says.

Two widths, no more: .wide (1040px) for a diagram that needs the room, .band (860px) for one that reads beside the prose. Everything else stays in the measure.

A drawing sits on the padding; a scan bleeds. Anything the system drew — an SVG diagram, a rendered canvas — sits inside the plate's padding, because its own whitespace was authored. A photograph or a scanned page runs to the plate's edge: it brought its own margins, and padding one only makes a frame inside a frame. The caption keeps its padding either way.
