A colour space with meaning

Nobody picks a pen colour here. You say what a thing is, and its colour follows from that kind and how it relates to your other kinds. Each axis of the colour space carries one meaning, so the colours on a board can be read back as words.

The example starts with the kinds you named on 2 October (ideas, tasks and notes) and adds the spec's examples of a family, kin and opposites. Add your own in the field. Every colour is computed in OKLCH and drawn as sRGB on both of the canvas's grounds.

Each cluster is ink of one kind, in its colour. An arrow between two things of the same family takes that family's colour, and one between different kinds stays ink. Patterns on the shapes are the second channel, given to kinds that someone could not tell apart by colour alone. Switching ground moves lightness and chroma; the hue never changes.

What the colours say

    The acts, in order

      The ledger

      On each ground, the top line is a kind you said, at full chroma. The line under it is the same kind offered, muted. Contrast is measured against the ground: words need 4.5:1 and marks 3:1.

      How this becomes KN3

      These functions move into core/src/colour/ with tests:

      • oklch.ts: the colour conversions, and fitting a colour inside sRGB by lowering its chroma.
      • scale.ts: lightness and chroma for each ground, depth and certainty.
      • harmony.ts: where a hue is placed.
      • access.ts: contrast, colour-blindness simulation and second channels.
      • names.ts: colour words.

      A kind's hue is chosen in the act that makes it or relates it, and that act keeps it. A kind added later never moves an older one. Lightness and chroma are worked out fresh for whichever ground is showing.

      The two grounds, their inks and the chrome's signal colours are brand/tokens.css's. The lightness bands are this page's until KN3 moves them into the tokens.

      What it found

      Spacing hues by angle is not enough. At a single lightness, someone with deuteranopia can lose two hues set 180° apart. So the space places each new hue where it is easiest to tell apart from the others. It checks typical sight first and colour-blind sight a close second, on both grounds.

      Past about eight kinds, the second channel does the rest. Your purple sits 9° from the violet the chrome uses to mark a model's work. That is fine, because the chrome's colours never go on the ink.
