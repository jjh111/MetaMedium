tap a tile, then draw

Choose a plane, then draw on it. The three tiles at the gizmo are the foundation (the ground), the height plane (the wall you face) and the width plane (the wall on your right). The chosen tile lights; the cone on its normal slides the plane along it; tapping the sphere at the centre un-chooses.

Draw with the left button or one finger. Orbit with the right button, a drag on the compass in the corner, or a drag with Space held. Pan with the middle button, Shift + the right button, or two fingers. The wheel or a pinch dollies toward the pointer, not toward the middle of the screen. An orbit turns around the centre of the view — where a pan left it — or around the selection when there is one, and that turn is rigid: nothing moves at the first pixel, and a view panned off-centre stays off-centre.

The compass in the top-right corner turns with the camera. Tap a ball to look along that axis — X is the right view, Y the top, Z the front, and the hollow balls are their opposites; tapping the ball you are already at flips to the other side. The ball that faces the chosen plane lights in the same teal as its tile, so one tap puts you flat on what you are drawing on. Drag anywhere on the compass to orbit — one finger, because it is chrome rather than canvas. Under it: home frames everything on the board, view swaps persp / ortho, and each pinned view is a chip.

Perspective follows the camera. A tap on a ball goes orthographic — a front, top or side view is a draftsman's, and in perspective two equal edges at different depths measure differently — and orbiting off the axis comes back to perspective, where depth is what says the thing is solid. Press the view tile and it stays where you put it until you tap a ball again. The tile says which it is either way.

Keys for the camera: the numpad, where Blender has it — 1 front, 3 right, 7 top, 5 persp/ortho, 9 the opposite side — or Shift + those digits on a keyboard without one, since plain 1 2 3 already choose a PLANE and a plane is chosen far more often than a camera is snapped. Ctrl/Cmd with either takes the opposite side, and f or Home frames everything.

Every stroke is projected onto its plane as it is drawn and stored in that plane's own coordinates, so the shape rung reads a drawing on the ground exactly as it reads one on paper — at the scale the pen was working at, which under perspective changes with depth.

Or don't choose, and the plane is read. Tap the sphere at the gizmo's centre and every stroke's plane becomes a reading: the faces under the pen, the plane you were drawing on a moment ago, the three world planes standing where the pen came down, and the plane facing you. It is fixed at pen-down from where the pen is and re-ranked at pen-up against the whole stroke — the first moment there is a stroke to read. The panel lists every candidate with its number and its reason, and the runner-up stands beside the mark as a chip: tap it and the ink is read onto that plane instead, one Cmd/Ctrl+Z back. A straight stroke reads the same on every plane, so a line rising off a profile is genuinely ambiguous — that is what the chip, and the tiles, are for.

Ink on the view plane keeps its view. It is sharp from the camera it was drawn at and faint from anywhere else; the panel's pinned views row takes you back.

Stand it up. A closed shape on a chosen plane is a profile. A line that starts on its edge and leaves its plane is an extent, and a box stands at once — tier 1, no model, no wait. A line lying beside the profile in its own plane is an axis, and the profile turns about it. The panel's plays row says which row of the form table placed each mark, and why.

The field is under the panel: type a verb (extrude, revolve, remove, undo) or name: …, and the line under it says what Enter will do before you press it.

Two or three views are a solid already. Draw a plan on the foundation, a front on the height plane and a side on the width plane; where their projections overlap is the massing, and it stands the moment the second one lands — tier 1, in the engine's name, with no word said. The drawing IS the extent.

Then say what it is. Type a castle with green turret tops in the field and press Enter: the reading line says → asks <model> first, and if none has joined it opens the model pane instead. What comes back is an op tree with its steps named from your own words and a colour word bound where you said one — never code, never a mesh. It is clipped to the massing before you ever see it, so a proposal cannot leave the drawing, and the panel says honours the drawing 93% · front 96 · top 95 · side 88. A model is asked only by that act; nothing on draw, nothing on join. Esc stops every call in flight, and a brief that fails leaves nothing behind.

Names are yours, and they conserve. Take it names the thing from the root step and holds every named sub-tree as a definition based on the whole, so typing turret afterwards completes from the library before any model is asked. Verbs then bind by name: make the turrets taller asks for those steps again and leaves every other step's id alone; remove the turret and the tops are red are tier 1 and instant. A phrasing the table cannot read is handed back, not dropped — the field offers to ask a model which of the space's own verbs you meant, once, and holds the answer in the log as a way of saying it.

Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes the last act — the solid if one stands, else the last stroke; the ink always stays. Hover a mark to read it in the panel; a tap on a solid selects it, Esc lets it go. Keys 1 2 3 choose a plane, 0 un-chooses; the camera's own keys are on the numpad, or Shift + the digit.
