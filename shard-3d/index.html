choose a plane · draw on it

Choose a plane, then draw on it. The gizmo's three tiles are the foundation (the ground), the height plane (the wall you face) and the width plane (the wall on your right). The chosen tile lights. The cone slides the plane along its normal; the centre sphere un-chooses.

Draw left button, one finger. Orbit right-drag, a drag on the compass, or Space+drag. Pan middle-drag, Shift+right-drag, two fingers. Dolly wheel or pinch, toward the pointer. An orbit turns about the view's centre — where a pan left it — or about the selection. Nothing snaps to the middle.

The compass turns with the camera. Tap a ball to look along that axis: X right, Y top, Z front, the hollow balls their opposites; tap the one you are at to flip. The ball facing the chosen plane lights teal, like its tile. Drag anywhere on it to orbit — one finger, because it is chrome. Under it: home frames everything, view is persp / ortho, each pinned view is a chip.

Perspective follows the camera. A ball goes ortho — in a front, top or side view perspective makes two equal edges at different depths measure differently — and orbiting off the axis returns to perspective, where depth says the thing is solid. Press view and it stays put until you tap a ball. The tile says which.

Camera keys are the numpad, where Blender has them: 1 front, 3 right, 7 top, 5 persp/ortho, 9 the far side. Without a numpad, Shift + the digit; Ctrl/Cmd takes the far side; f or Home frames. Plain 1 2 3 choose a PLANE and 0 un-chooses — a plane is chosen far more often than a camera is snapped.

Every stroke is stored in its plane's own coordinates, so the shape rung reads a drawing on the ground as it reads one on paper — at the pen's scale, which under perspective changes with depth.

Or don't choose, and the plane is read. Tap the gizmo's centre sphere and every stroke's plane becomes a reading: the faces under the pen, the plane you last drew on, the three world planes at the pen, the plane facing you. It is fixed at pen-down and re-ranked at pen-up against the whole stroke. The panel lists each candidate with its number and its reason; the runner-up stands beside the mark as a chip — tap it and the ink is read onto that plane instead, one Cmd/Ctrl+Z back. A straight stroke reads the same on every plane, which is what the chip and the tiles are for.

Ink on the view plane keeps its view — sharp from the camera it was drawn at, faint from anywhere else. The panel's pinned views row takes you back.

Stand it up. A closed shape on a chosen plane is a profile. A line from its edge, off its plane, is an extent, and a box stands at once — tier 1, no model, no wait. A line beside it in its own plane is an axis, and the profile turns about it. The panel's plays row says which row of the form table placed each mark, and why.

The field is under the panel: type a verb (extrude, revolve, remove, undo) or name: …. The line under it says what Enter will do.

Two or three views are a solid already. A plan on the foundation, a front on the height plane, a side on the width plane: where they overlap is the massing, and it stands the moment the second lands — tier 1, in the engine's name, with no word said. The drawing IS the extent.

Then say what it is. Type a castle with green turret tops and press Enter. The reading line says → asks <model> first; with none joined it opens the model pane. Back comes an op tree with its steps named from your own words and a colour word bound where you said one — never code, never a mesh. It is clipped to the massing before you see it, so a proposal cannot leave the drawing, and the panel says honours the drawing 93% · front 96 · top 95 · side 88. A model is asked only by that act. Esc stops every call in flight; a brief that fails leaves nothing behind.

Names are yours, and they conserve. Take it names the thing from the root step and holds every named sub-tree as a definition based on the whole, so typing turret completes from the library before any model is asked. Verbs then bind by name: make the turrets taller asks for those steps again and leaves every other step's id alone; remove the turret and the tops are red are tier 1 and instant. A phrasing the table cannot read is handed back, not dropped — the field offers to ask a model which of the space's own verbs you meant, once, and holds the answer.

Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes the last act — the solid if one stands, else the last stroke; the ink always stays. Hover a mark to read it in the panel; tap a solid to select it, Esc to let it go.
